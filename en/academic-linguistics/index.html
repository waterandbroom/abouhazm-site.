What you will explore

Phonetics

How speech sounds are produced and heard. Why it matters: it is the base of clear pronunciation and the key to reading any transcription.

Phonology

How sounds work as a system: why “ship” and “sheep” are two different words, not just two different sounds.

Morphology

How words are built from smaller parts, and how new words are born.

Syntax

How words combine into sentences, and why some sentences simply feel wrong.

Semantics and pragmatics

How meaning is built, and how context changes what we really mean.

Applied and social linguistics

How languages are learned, and how they vary between people and places.

A quick taste: phonetics

Take a single symbol: /ə/, the schwa. It is the most frequent vowel sound in English, yet it is rarely heard and often mispronounced. Learning to hear it changes your rhythm, your listening and your accent. This is the kind of understanding you get in a lesson: not a list of definitions, but an idea that stays with you.
